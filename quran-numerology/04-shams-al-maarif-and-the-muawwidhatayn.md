# Shams al-Ma'arif and the Mu'awwidhatayn: connections, insights and the dividing line

Part 4 of the study. Parts [1](01-surah-al-falaq.md), [2](02-al-falaq-counter-pattern.md) and
[3](03-an-nas-and-the-pair.md) analysed Al-Falaq and An-Nas: their numbers, their structure as a counter-pattern to
sorcery, and their working as a pair. This part brings in ***Shams al-Ma'arif al-Kubra***, the most famous manual of
Islamic letter-and-number science (*'ilm al-huruf*). It works in two directions:

1. **The book's lens on the surahs.** What do the book's own classifications reveal when applied to Al-Falaq and
   An-Nas?
2. **The surahs' lens on the book.** Where do the Prophetic protection and the book's methods meet, and where do
   they part?

Tags as before: **[Fact]** computed or documented · **[Tradition]** classical scholarship or hadith ·
**[Reflection]** interpretation. A further tag, **[Lettrist]**, marks a result that is only meaningful *inside* the
letter-science system. That system's classifications have no basis in the Quran or Sunnah, so these results describe
how the tradition *would read* the text. They are not findings about the text's meaning.

---

## 0. Summary

- **Common ground.** The numerology developed in Parts 1–3 uses the **same abjad system** that Shams al-Ma'arif uses.
  Every value we computed (777, 1111, 241/142, 142 = 142) can be read in the book's framework.
- **The book's lens yields real descriptive patterns:**
  - Al-Falaq, the surah of darkness, is the **"darkest" surah of the three Quls** by the book's light/dark letter
    classification. Its 20 distinct letters split **exactly 10 luminous and 10 dark**.
  - **Each evil named in Al-Falaq carries a "dark" letter that appears nowhere else in the surah.**
  - In An-Nas, the verses naming Allah as **King** and **God** are made **entirely of luminous letters**.
  - Al-Fatiha contains all 14 luminous letters. The 7 dark letters it lacks reappear, mostly, in the two refuge
    surahs, where evil is finally named.
- **The surahs' lens on the book shows a clear dividing line.** The book and the Mu'awwidhatayn use the same raw
  material: letters, numbers, divine names, the moon and the planets. They use it in **opposite directions**:

  | | Shams al-Ma'arif | The Mu'awwidhatayn |
  |---|---|---|
  | The structure is | the **instrument** | the **witness** |
  | Medium | written | spoken |
  | Access | secret | public |
  | Address | intermediaries, plus the names | Allah alone |
  | The moon and planets are | sources of influence | created things one seeks refuge *from* |

- **What this part does not do.** It does not build magic squares (*awfaq*), talismans or "servant" (*khadim*) names
  from these surahs. That would mean practising the tradition rather than studying it. It would also, by the
  criteria in §4, turn the counter-pattern back into the pattern (Part 2 §6).

## 1. The book **[Fact: documented scholarship]**

| | |
|---|---|
| Traditional author | **Ahmad ibn 'Ali al-Buni** (d. 622/1225), a Sufi of North African origin who worked in Egypt. Al-Zabidi calls him the master of "the Bunian way in names and letters". |
| What the printed book actually is | A **compilation by several hands**. It includes some genuine al-Buni material, but recent scholarship dates the text in its familiar form to around the **17th century** (Edgar Francis; Noah Gardiner on the "Corpus Bunianum"). |
| Editions and manuscripts | Printed as *al-Kubra* (larger) and *al-Sughra*. Before print, three versions of different lengths circulated. There is **no critical edition**. Paris BnF MS Arabe 2650–51 is among the earliest complete copies. |
| Contents | About **40 chapters**: the secrets of the letters and the abjad; the divine names and their properties (*khawass*); **magic squares**; **talismans** and the "Seven Seals"; astrological timing; the **lunar mansions** and their influences; the letters mapped onto the celestial spheres and the **12 zodiac signs**. |
| Reception | **Ibn Khaldun** (*Muqaddimah*, ch. VI) calls the science of letter secrets ***simiya'***. He says it arose "after the first generations" with the **extremist Sufis**, and names **al-Buni and Ibn 'Arabi** as its main authors. He groups it with talismans and sorcery. Many later scholars condemn the book as containing sorcery. It nonetheless circulates widely in folk practice, from West Africa to South Asia. |
| Sympathetic readings | Studies of al-Buni's *authentic* works (Francis 2005; Lien Iffah Naf'atu Fina) argue that his letter science was rooted in Sufi devotion (*dhikr*, fasting, the divine names). Much of the cruder magic in the printed *Shams* belongs to later layers. |

**[Reflection]** For this project, the book matters because the idea that **numbers and letters arranged in the right
pattern can protect** is historically this book's territory. Number grids (*naqsh*) on amulets, abjad-derived recitation
counts and "786" for the Basmala all descend from the tradition the *Shams* came to represent.

## 2. The book's lens on the two surahs

### 2.1 Luminous and dark letters
**[Lettrist]** Letter science divides the 28 letters into two sets of 14:

| Set | Letters | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Luminous** (*nurani*) | ا ل م ر ص ط س ك ه ي ع ح ق ن | the 14 letters of the disjointed openings of surahs (*al-muqatta'at*, such as الم and كهيعص) |
| **Dark** (*zulmani*) | ب ت ث ج خ د ذ ز ش ض ظ غ ف و | the other 14 |

The tradition reserves the luminous letters for "works of good". The "luminous" set itself is a **[Fact]**: these 14
really are the letters of the muqatta'at.

**[Fact]** Computed with that classification:

| Text | Share of luminous letters (all letters counted) | Distinct letters: luminous / dark |
|---|---|---|
| Whole Quran | 74.8% | 14 / 14 |
| Al-Fatiha | **85.6%** | **14** / 7 |
| Al-Ikhlas | 76.6% | 10 / 3 |
| **Al-Falaq** | **64.8%**, the lowest of these | **10 / 10** |
| **An-Nas** | **80.0%** | 12 / 8 |

What the lettrist reading draws from this:

- **Al-Falaq sits exactly on the line between light and dark.** **[Fact]** Its 20 distinct letters split 10/10, and
  it has the lowest luminous share of the three Quls and Al-Fatiha. **[Reflection]** The surah about the boundary
  between night and daybreak is, in the tradition's own terms, letter-for-letter balanced on that boundary.
- **Light letters frame the surah; dark letters carry the three hidden evils.** **[Fact]** Verses 1 (the refuge) and
  2 (all creation) end on **ق**, a luminous letter. Verses 3–5 (darkness, sorcery, envy) end on **ب د د**, all dark.
- **Each evil has its own dark letter.** **[Fact]** Every evil named in Al-Falaq contains a dark letter that occurs
  **nowhere else in the surah**:

  | Evil | Its exclusive dark letter |
  |---|---|
  | شَرّ ("evil" itself) | **ش** |
  | مَا **خ**َلَقَ (all creation) | **خ** |
  | **غ**َاسِق (darkness) | **غ** |
  | ٱلنَّفَّٰ**ثٰت** (the blowers) | **ث** and **ت** |
  | ٱلْعُقَ**د** · حَاسِ**د** · حَسَ**د** (knots, envy) | **د** |

  **[Reflection]** Read lettristically, each evil is "signed" with its own dark letter. Each verse names that evil,
  isolates it, and places it under the refuge.
- **In An-Nas, the names of the Protector are pure light.** **[Fact]** Verses 2 and 3, *Maliki n-nas* and *Ilahi
  n-nas*, consist **100% of luminous letters** (8 of 8 each). The word **الناس** is itself entirely luminous, and all
  six verses end on the luminous **س**. An-Nas's dark letters fall only in the opening formula (أعوذ برب) and in
  verses 4–6, which describe the threat: شر, الوسواس, الخناس, الذى يوسوس فى صدور, الجنة.
- **Al-Fatiha holds the light; the refuge surahs name the dark.** **[Fact]**
  - Al-Fatiha contains **all 14 luminous letters**. The 7 letters it famously lacks, **ث ج خ ز ش ظ ف**, are all
    dark.
  - Al-Falaq and An-Nas together supply **5 of those 7** (ث ج خ ش ف).
  - The three surahs the Sunnah uses for ruqya (Al-Fatiha, Al-Falaq and An-Nas) together use every letter except
    **ز** and **ظ**.

  **[Reflection]** The book that opens in light names the darkness only at its end, inside the surahs of refuge.
  It names it in order to seek refuge from it. **[Lettrist]** A letter-scientist would add that **ظ**, the initial
  of *zulumat* (darknesses) and *zulm* (wrongdoing), appears in none of the three protective surahs.

### 2.2 The four elemental groups
**[Lettrist]** The tradition assigns the letters to the four elements by cycling through the abjad order:

| Element | Letters |
|---|---|
| Fire | ا ه ط م ف ش ذ |
| Air | ب و ي ن ص ت ض |
| Water | ج ز ك س ق ث ظ |
| Earth | د ح ل ع ر خ غ |

This is the widespread table. I could not verify the exact page of the printed *Shams*, and some Maghribi variants
differ.

**[Fact]** Shares computed with this table:

| Text | Fire | Air | Water | Earth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whole Quran | 35.8% | 32.1% | **9.4%** | 22.6% |
| Al-Falaq | 36.6% | 21.1% | **14.1%** | 28.2% |
| An-Nas | 33.8% | 27.5% | **16.2%** | 22.5% |

- Both refuge surahs are **richer in "water" letters** than the Quran as a whole, about 1.5–1.7×. Al-Falaq's
  signature letter **ق** and An-Nas's signature letter **س** are both "water" letters.
- **[Reflection]** In the Quran, water is life ("We made from water every living thing", 21:30). It is also the
  explicit remover of Satan's defilement: "He sent down water from the sky **to purify you and remove from you the
  defilement of Satan**" (8:11). The match is pretty, but it hangs on a classification the Quran itself never uses.
  Treat it as ornament, not argument.

### 2.3 The abjad: one system, two uses
**[Fact]** Every value in Parts 1–3 comes from the eastern abjad, the same system the *Shams* uses:

| Value | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| **777** | أعوذ |
| **1111** | the shared opening قل أعوذ برب |
| **241 / 142** | الفلق and الناس, mirror numbers |
| **142 = 142** | يوسوس and الناس |
| **241** | الناس occurs 241 times in the Quran |

**[Tradition]** In the book's practice, a value like this becomes an *operational* number. It sets how many times to
recite a name (a common example is reciting *Ya Latif* 129 times, the abjad of لطيف). It can also be the target sum of
a magic square, or the seed for generating a "servant's" name.

**[Reflection]** The same numbers that, in Parts 1–3, served as *evidence of design* would here become *settings of a
device*. The Sunnah's counts for these surahs are **not** derived from abjad. They are 1 after each prayer, 3 in the
morning, evening and at bedtime, and the 7 attached to *a'udhu* (Muslim 2202). Nothing in the hadith tells you to
recite Al-Falaq 241 or 777 times.

### 2.4 The moon and the planets
- **[Fact]** The *Shams* builds much of its operation on the **28 lunar mansions** and the **planets**: when to write,
  what each station influences. Letter science (including Ibn 'Arabi) pairs the **28 letters with the 28 lunar
  mansions**.
- **[Fact]** The Mu'awwidhatayn name these very bodies:
  - **The moon** is *al-ghasiq idha waqab*. The Prophet ﷺ pointed at it and said: "**Seek refuge in Allah from the
    evil of this**" (Tirmidhi 3366).
  - **The receding planets** share An-Nas's root: *al-khunnas* (81:15), from the same root as *al-khannas*.
- **[Reflection]** This is the sharpest point of contact, and the sharpest inversion:

  | | The occult system | The refuge surahs |
  |---|---|---|
  | The moon and planets are | **sources of influence**, to be timed and harnessed | **created things** under *Rabb al-Falaq*; the moon is something to seek refuge *from* |

  The Quran states the principle outright: "Do not prostrate to the sun or to the moon, but prostrate to Allah who
  **created them**" (41:37). Ibrahim's turn away from the star, the moon and the sun, "**I do not love things that
  set**" (6:76), has the same shape.
- **[Tradition]** "Whoever acquires a branch of knowledge of the stars has acquired a branch of sorcery"
  (Abu Dawud 3905).

## 3. Where the two genuinely meet

These points are shared, and both traditions hold them:

| Shared conviction | In mainstream Sunni sources | In the *Shams* / al-Buni |
|---|---|---|
| The letters of revelation are not ordinary | "Whoever recites a letter of Allah's Book has a reward… *Alif Lam Mim* is not one letter; *alif* is a letter, *lam* a letter, *mim* a letter" (Tirmidhi 2910) | letters as carriers of divine "secrets" |
| The Quran heals | "We send down of the Quran what is **healing** and mercy" (17:82) | *khawass*, the properties of verses |
| The divine names are the core of protection | *Rabb, Malik, Ilah* in An-Nas; *Rabb al-Falaq* | the genuine al-Buni stratum centres on the 99 names |
| The cosmos is an ordered system of signs | the heavens are "guarded"; the sun and moon run "by calculation" (55:5) | correspondences between letters, spheres and mansions |
| Light against darkness | Al-Falaq's daybreak against *ghasiq*; "Allah is the light of the heavens and earth" (24:35) | luminous and dark letters |
| Protection is a structured, repeated practice | fixed counts and times (Part 2 §7) | prescribed counts, times and conditions |

**[Reflection]** These overlaps explain why the lettrist impulse attached itself to the Quran in the first place.
The Mu'awwidhatayn really **are** structured, as Parts 1–3 show in detail. The disagreement is not *whether* the
structure exists. It is about **what to do with it**.

## 4. Where they part: the dividing line

**[Tradition]** Ibn Hajar (*Fath al-Bari* 10/195) reports agreement that ruqya is permitted when **three conditions**
are met:
1. It is by **Allah's speech, or His names and attributes**.
2. It is in **Arabic, or a language whose meaning is understood**.
3. The person believes it **does not work by itself, but by Allah's power**.

He also notes some disagreement over whether these are strict conditions. Underlying them all is the hadith "There is
no harm in ruqya as long as it contains no shirk" (Muslim 2200).

Measured against these conditions:

| Element | *Shams al-Ma'arif* methods | The Mu'awwidhatayn | Condition at stake |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Words** | Quranic verses and divine names, **but also obscure names and formulas** (e.g. the Jaljalutiyya, the "Seven Seals") | only Allah's speech, in clear Arabic; it opens with "**Say**" | 1 and 2 |
| **Address** | divine names, but also **spiritual intermediaries** and celestial influences | **Allah alone**: *a'udhu bi-Rabb* (refuge with jinn "increased them in fear", 72:6) | 1 |
| **Medium** | **written**: squares, seals, talismans, worn or placed | **spoken, breathed, wiped** (Bukhari 5017) | (amulets; see below) |
| **Mechanism** | the **properties** (*khawass*) of letters, numbers and timings | **Allah's protection**, sought through refuge | 3 |
| **Timing** | **planetary hours and lunar stations** | **the daily cycle and the prayers**: dawn, dusk, after salah, bedtime | Abu Dawud 3905 |
| **Counts** | **derived from abjad values** | **from the Sunnah**: 1, 3, 7 | — |
| **Access** | **esoteric**, passed down through initiation | **public**: taught to every Muslim, recited over children | — |
| **Celestial bodies** | sources of power | created things; refuge *from* their evil (113:3, Tirmidhi 3366) | 41:37 |

**Fairness notes.**
- The *Shams* is not uniform. Its Quran-and-names material would meet conditions 1–2. Its talismanic and astrological
  material is what most scholars reject.
- Scholars **differed** over amulets that contain **only Quran**. Some early authorities allowed them; Ibn Mas'ud and
  others forbade them. There is much broader agreement against amulets with **obscure names** or appeals to anything
  besides Allah (Abu Dawud 3883; Tirmidhi 2072, "whoever hangs something is entrusted to it").

**[Tradition]** The historical endpoint is already in the hadith. Before the Mu'awwidhatayn, the Prophet ﷺ used various
formulas against the jinn and the eye. **"When they were revealed, he took them and left everything else"**
(Tirmidhi 2058). In the Prophet's own practice, the Mu'awwidhatayn are the **last word on protective formulas**. In
Ibn Khaldun's history, the letter science came **later**, "after the first generations".

## 5. The central insight: same material, opposite direction

```
                SHAMS AL-MA'ARIF                          AL-FALAQ + AN-NAS
   letters ──► hidden properties ──► device      letters ──► meaning ──► address to Allah
   numbers ──► square / count / seal            numbers ──► evidence of design, for reflection
   moon, planets ──► influences to harness      moon, receding stars ──► creatures; refuge FROM them
   names of spirits ──► servants to bind        jinn ──► a source of the whisper (114:6)
   secret, written, initiatory                  "Say!": spoken, public, for everyone
   structure = INSTRUMENT                       structure = WITNESS
```

Part 2 found that Al-Falaq inverts the anatomy of sorcery: binding into splitting, darkness into daybreak, hidden into
spoken, refuge with jinn into refuge with the Lord. **Shams al-Ma'arif is the test case for that finding.**

- It is the most elaborate attempt in Islamic history to build protection out of the **properties** of letters,
  numbers and stars.
- Set beside it, the Mu'awwidhatayn show that the Quran *has* the letter and number architecture the lettrists were
  looking for, as Parts 1, 3 and §2 here show.
- The Quran places that architecture **behind** the act of refuge, never **in front of** it.

**[Reflection]** The structure tells you the text was built with care. The refuge is what actually protects you.

## 6. Using this in your project

**Legitimate uses:**
- Use the book's classifications **as a descriptive lens**, labelled as "how the letter-science tradition would read
  this". Examples: the luminous/dark split, the element shares, the abjad values. They yield genuinely striking
  observations (§2.1).
- Present the abjad findings of Parts 1–3 as **evidence of design and aids to reflection**: 777, 1111, the 241/142
  mirror, 142 = 142.
- Use the comparison in §4 to explain to readers **why** the Prophetic method looks the way it does: spoken, clear,
  public, God-addressed and timed by prayer.

**Lines this study does not cross:**
- Building **awfaq** (magic squares) from these surahs' values, generating **khadim** names, or prescribing
  **abjad-derived** recitation counts.
- Each of these takes the surahs' structure out of the "witness" column of §5 and into the "instrument" column. By
  the criteria in §4, that is the step most scholars regard as crossing from ruqya into *simiya'* or sorcery.

## 7. Strength of each claim

| Strength | Findings |
|---|---|
| **Documented fact** | the book's attribution, dating and contents; Ibn Khaldun's classification; Ibn Hajar's three conditions; the hadith cited |
| **Computed fact** (classification-dependent) | Al-Falaq's 10/10 luminous/dark split and lowest luminous share; ق endings on vv. 1–2 and dark endings on vv. 3–5; each evil's exclusive dark letter; An-Nas vv. 2–3 entirely luminous; Al-Fatiha's 14 luminous letters and the 5-of-7 complement in the refuge surahs; higher "water" share |
| **Lettrist only** | any *meaning* drawn from luminous/dark or elemental classes; the ظ = *zulumat* remark |
| **Reflection** | instrument vs witness; the inversion of the moon and planets; Al-Fatiha holding the light and the refuge surahs naming the dark |
